§02 — The proof · scroll to construct

Every proof begins as a guess.

  1. 01Given a triangle.Sharp, flat or lopsided. The result won’t care.
  2. 02Mark the midpoints.Each side cut exactly in half. The ticks say which halves match.
  3. 03Join each vertex to the opposite midpoint.Three lines, drawn separately. The guess: do they all pass through one point?
  4. 04They do. Every time.The centroid G sits two-thirds of the way down each median. Now prove it.

Find all f : ℝ → ℝ with f(x + y) = f(x) + f(y) and f(xy) = f(x)f(y) for all real x, y.

ARAnanya R.Approach 1

Put y = 1 in the second: f(x)(1 − f(1)) = 0. So either f ≡ 0, or f(1) = 1.

31 votes4 repliesSPLIT CASES
IMIshaan M.Approach 2

Put y = x: f(x2) = f(x)2 ≥ 0. So f is non-negative on non-negatives, and additivity makes it increasing.

Additivity gives f(q) = q on the rationals. An increasing function that agrees with x on ℚ must be f(x) = x.

§08 — Why we built Liminal

We didn’t build a coaching centre. We built the room we wished we’d had.

Most of us met olympiad mathematics alone, with a borrowed book and nobody to ask. The hard part was never the problems. It was having no one to think out loud with.

Liminal is that room: a problem worth a morning, people who care how you got there, and the patience to stay in the in-between until it makes sense.
