A maths degree is a licence, not a job title

Employers are not buying the theorems you memorised. They are buying the speed at which you will learn their problem. What the market rewards is mathematics plus one.

IMO participants never earn a maths PhD. A medal is a signal with an expiry date: it opens the first interview, and by your second job nobody asks. The medal is the receipt. The training is the purchase.

A plan by stage

Where to start.

Not a script, but a sequence that keeps the most doors open for the longest.

  1. 1Classes 8–10Build the habitWrite proofs, try the IOQM early, start one programming language.
  2. 2Classes 11–12Climb, don’t drillTake the ladder seriously and learn the admission routes. Understanding compounds; drilling fades.
  3. 3Undergraduate 1–2Core plus oneAnalysis, algebra, linear algebra, probability. Choose your “plus one” early.
  4. 4Undergraduate 3–4Test the watersOne research project, one internship. Decide PhD or industry by final year.
  5. 5After the degreeSpecialise deliberatelyA master’s or PhD is a decision, not a default. Keep your maths alive.
